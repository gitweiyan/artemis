# iOS UVC P11: PointerControl — skills on a phone without absolute touch

> **REQUIRED SUB-SKILL:** superpowers:executing-plans.

**Goal:** `play_reflex` (and therefore any reflex skill, e.g. Tetris) can drive the iOS rig.
Skills ask for absolute `Gesture.tap(x, y)` / `Gesture.swipe(...)` in device pixels; iOS
only accepts a relative pointer, so the adapter must travel, verify, click and confirm the
screen changed — never blind-tap.

**Architecture:** `artemis/reflex/pointer_control.py` implements the `Control` protocol on
top of `SessionTapController` + `ScreenGeometry`. `IOSUvcHidDriver.pointer_control()` binds
one to its own sink, and `play_reflex` selects it by capability
(`supports_absolute_tap is False`), not by platform name.

**Spec:** `docs/ios-uvc-execution-plan.md` Phase 5 (IOS-UVC-53), target doc §4.3/§5.

## Global Constraints

- No click without a verified pointer; a refused tap raises (the reflex runtime counts
  consecutive failures and hands control back).
- Coordinates: skills speak device pixels; the controller speaks capture-frame pixels.
- A drag must not be reported as successful unless the screen changed.

## Review Focus

- A skill that emits a tap outside the screen must fail loudly, not clamp silently.
- Swipes must go press → segmented moves → release even when an intermediate move fails.
- The adapter must be usable with a fake session (skill development stays offline).
- `play_reflex` keeps its Android paths untouched.
